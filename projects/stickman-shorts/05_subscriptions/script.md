# AI Roommates ep3: "Subscriptions" (EN, draft v1)

Topic: small monthly subscriptions vs the same money put into an index fund every month.
Same HERO and ROOMMATE (MASTER C reference). Same formula: strange act → mocker → rival "like a normal
person" → payoff on screen → "See, …" + everyday analogy → ironic ending.
Numbers: $60/month × 10 years = $7,200 paid in; at an average 7%/year that grows to ~$10,400
(monthly compounding). On-screen footnote: "Not financial advice. Returns aren't guaranteed."

## Narration (~175 words, ~62 s)
On the first of every month, he moves sixty dollars somewhere… and never touches it again.
No streaming. No music app. No premium anything.
His roommate laughs: "You're twenty-two, not sixty. Live a little!"
The roommate has nine subscriptions. Three streaming services, music, a gym he went to twice, and an app that reminds him to drink water. Sixty dollars a month. He stopped noticing them years ago.
Ten years go by.
The roommate has paid seven thousand two hundred dollars… to rewatch the same three shows.
See, the guy didn't do anything clever. He put that money into an index fund — a basket of hundreds of companies at once. And the money grows on the money it already made.
Like a snowball rolling downhill: at first it barely moves, then it picks up everything in its way.
Seven thousand two hundred went in. About ten thousand four hundred came out — if the market averages seven percent a year. It never promises that.
So at thirty-two, one of them books a trip around the world with money he "never had".
And the other one gets an email: "Your gym membership has been renewed."
