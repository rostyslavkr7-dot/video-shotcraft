July, 1518. Strasbourg. A woman named Frau Troffea steps out into the street… and starts to dance.
No music. No reason. For an hour. A day. Six days straight.
Within a week, thirty more people join her. Within a month — hundreds.
They dance until their feet bleed. They collapse… and get right back up.
The doctors call a council and make their diagnosis: “hot blood.” The cure? Dance even more.
So the city does something unbelievable. It builds a wooden stage. Hires musicians. Pays strong men to hold the dancers on their feet.
The music plays day and night. And the dancers… only multiply.
Finally, the authorities give up. Music is banned. The dancers are carted up into the mountains, to the shrine of Saint Vitus.
There, they’re given little red shoes… and prayed over.
And by September, the epidemic simply vanishes.
What it really was — no one knows to this day.
